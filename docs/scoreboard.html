Turn an expert's screen walkthrough into requirements a team can build from

An expert shares their screen and talks through a process. specto listens, looks at what was on screen at each moment, and writes the requirements, the acceptance criteria and the questions nobody answered. This page shows how well it does that, what it saves, and where it goes next.

What a transcript alone would have missed

In the onboarding reading, five of the seventeen questions came from looking at the screens, not from the words: the Approve button visible to someone the expert said cannot approve; a record submittable while its ID is still "Pending check"; a queue labelled "oldest first" with the approved row at the bottom; a search returning a different postcode from the one typed; a proof-of-address upload the expert never mentioned. These are the questions that cost a sprint when found late.

What is measured and what is assumed

Measured

  • Recall against answer keys: 0.97 and 1.00 on the onboarding example, 1.00 on the complaints example
  • Screens caught: 6 of 6 with the fingerprint detector, 1 of 6 with the brightness detector
  • Model cost estimate: $1.15 to $1.60 per recorded hour on Claude Opus 5, from token counts of a synthetic hour with 40 screens
  • Preparation time: about 20 seconds per recorded hour at full size
  • Local speech-to-text: 4% word error on the example audio

Assumed, and how to replace it

  • Hours to write up a recording by hand: time three analysts on one recording
  • Hours to review a specto draft: time the same analysts on the draft
  • Readings so far used Claude acting through a session, not the paid API: the first API run replaces this with a bill
  • Both examples are built for the purpose, with narration and screens designed to be readable: real recordings will be messier and the scores will move
Source and reference results: github.com/patkusch/specto. Every figure marked measured is reproducible from the repository: the answer keys, the reference readings and the stress script are all there.
